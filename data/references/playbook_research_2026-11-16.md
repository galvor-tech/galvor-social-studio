# B2B marketing playbook research: Clay, Motion, Ramp, Lovable

Compiled 16 Nov 2026 for the Galvor marketing strategy slide.

## How to read this file

- **Opened** = I opened the page on 16 Nov 2026 and the fact is on it.
- **Search only** = the fact appeared in a search-result summary; the page itself was not opened or was blocked. Treat as a lead, not a citation.
- **Company-claimed** = the number comes from the company or its staff, even when printed by a third party.
- **Independent** = reported or measured by someone other than the company.
- **Judgement** = my inference, not a sourced fact.
- Pages were read through an automated summariser, so quoted wording should be checked against the page before it goes on a slide verbatim.
- Revenue and valuation figures are included only with a named outlet and date.

### The plays

**1. "GTM Engineer" job title and category**
- What: Clay named an internal hybrid role (part SDR, AE and sales engineer) that grew out of live "reverse demos", then promoted it as an industry career with essays, a jobs narrative and a scholarship.
- Evidence (company-claimed): "400+ GTME jobs were posted this spring at a $160K median salary", attributed by Clay to Pave. Source: Clay Series C page (opened) https://clay.com/series-c , undated, round announced 5 Aug 2025. A $1M GTM-engineer scholarship fund was announced with the Series D (Clay blog, 9 Sep 2026).
- Evidence (independent, and it cuts the other way): Kyle Poyar's Growth Unhinged piece "Do you need a GTM engineer?" (10 Sep 2025, https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/do-you-need-a-gtm-engineer ) is subtitled as an "uneven rise"; a Substack note by Poyar reports 128 GTM Engineer postings over three months. Search only; the article is paywalled and the date window is not confirmed.
- Not verified: any neutral count of people holding the title.
- Judgement: the title worked because Clay already had hundreds of paying agencies and power users whose income depended on the skill. The name gave an existing group a flag; it did not create the group.

**2. Agencies first, then a partner ecosystem**
- What: Varun Anand narrowed early prospects to cold-email agency owners, joined their WhatsApp and Slack groups, ran up to eight 30-minute reverse demos a day, and ended each by moving the customer into Clay's Slack (Intercom support was removed). Later formalised as the Solutions Partner programme and Clay Experts.
- Evidence (company-claimed): early on about seven demos to close a $200-300/month customer, later one or none. Source: First Round Review (opened). Agencies built on Clay reaching "$1M+ ARR in under a year"; one named agency at ">$3M ARR with a 22 person team". Source: Clay Series C page (opened).
- Not verified: "108+" or "125+" agencies (search only, LinkedIn post and a job listing).

**3. Creators programme on LinkedIn**
- What: Clay activated existing users who already posted about it, in three tiers (Creators, Clay Experts, top RevOps agencies). Creators get affiliate links, branded screen recordings, templates, paid promotion of top posts, Clay credits and LinkedIn ad spend.
- Evidence (company-claimed): "Exclusive Slack community of 300+ Clay creators". Source: Clay creators page (opened) https://www.clay.com/partners/creators , undated, page marked under construction on 16 Nov 2026. Tier structure: Exit Five write-up of Bruno Estrella (opened) https://exitfive.com/clay-marketing-strategy/ , undated. Revenue share on deals plus badges: MKT1 (opened), 30 Jan 2025. Bruno Estrella: "The revenue is honestly an afterthought for them" (MKT1).
- By Jul 2026 Clay described its influencer use as "a handful of vetted B2B operators, not paid broadly" (Clay blog, 10 Jul 2026).
- Not verified: "20% revenue share for 12 months" and "200+ creators" (search only, LinkedIn posts); "8K to 40K LinkedIn followers in 10 months" appears only as an Exit Five section heading with no supporting detail.

**4. Education: Clay University, certifications, cohorts, bootcamps**
- What: free curriculum and certifications, live cohort courses, later enterprise cohorts (operators from Qualtrics, Wix, UiPath named). Third parties now run their own bootcamps.
- Evidence (company-claimed): "Clay's official cohort program has 2,500 alumni"; seven independent bootcamps teach GTM engineering. Source: Clay Series C page (opened), Aug 2025.
- Current state (search only): Clay's certifications page says new certification submissions are paused while the programme is rebuilt (https://www.clay.com/university/certifications ). Worth knowing before copying: certification programmes need upkeep.
- Not verified: number of certified users or University learners.

**5. Community: Slack and Clay Clubs**
- What: a Slack workspace doubling as support, plus volunteer-run in-person user groups.
- Evidence (company-claimed): "60+ Clay clubs across more than 30 countries" (Clay Series C page, opened, Aug 2025; First Round Review also says 60 clubs).
- Slack size: Emily Kramer of MKT1 said on 30 Jan 2025 "I believe you have something like 18,000 people in there". This is the interviewer's estimate, not a Clay figure.
- Not verified: "30k Slack members" and "84+ clubs" (search only, job listings).

**6. Executive and employee LinkedIn programme**
- What: each executive owns content for their domain. Davide Grieco's stated post mix: promotional 20%, personal 25-40%, thought leadership 40%, humour 0-15%.
- Evidence (company-claimed): "6 million organic impressions in a single quarter" from the executive programme; one post by Davide "drove over 4,500 signups", which Clay values at $300,000-400,000 in paid-ad equivalent. Source: Clay blog (opened), 10 Jul 2026.

**7. "How Clay Uses Clay" livestreams and user-generated content loop**
- What: a recurring livestream showing Clay's own team using the product; users posting their builds on LinkedIn; "Clayback", a yearly Spotify-Wrapped-style recap per user.
- Evidence (company-claimed): the livestream series drew "around 19,000 registrants at a blended cost per lead under $12", more than a third of signups from social. Source: Clay blog (opened), 10 Jul 2026.

**8. Brand and design as an early investment**
- What: a head of brand among the first 25 employees and a five-person brand team at 18-20 staff; a full-time claymation artist; event stunts (perfumery workshop, "spa day" at Dreamforce 2024); billboards in San Francisco and New York with the line "Every artist has their medium, go-to-market has Clay"; Gong call mentions used to tie billboards back to pipeline.
- Evidence: Clay's leaders say they "probably over-invest" in brand. Sources: HubSpot for Startups magazine (opened), 2025; First Round Review (opened); Clay blog (opened), 10 Jul 2026. No brand-lift number is published.

**9. Events**
- What: Sculpt annual conference (second edition in 2026), "GTM in GMT" London conference, pipeline dinners.
- Evidence: no attendance figures published. Source: Clay blog Series D post (opened), 9 Sep 2026; Clay blog, 10 Jul 2026.

**10. Templates ("Claybooks")**
- Not verified. I found no Clay-published number for template usage or signups from templates.

### Cost for a 1-2 person team to imitate (judgement)

| Play | Effort | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse demos that end in a shared channel | Low | Founder time only; works at 10 customers |
| Executive/employee LinkedIn with a fixed post mix | Low to medium | 3-5 hours a week per poster; needs founders who will post |
| "How we use our own product" livestream | Medium | One session a month is feasible; Clay's registrant numbers depend on an existing audience |
| Creators programme | Medium to high | Only works once outside users already post about the product unprompted |
| Job-title or category creation | High | Needs a base of practitioners who earn from the skill; premature for a tiny following |
| University, certifications, cohorts | High | Curriculum plus ongoing upkeep; Clay itself has paused certification submissions |
| Clubs, conference, billboards, brand team | Very high | Budget and headcount plays; reject for now |

## 2. Motion (motionapp.com; site now redirects to runneth.com)

Relevance: Motion sells creative analytics to D2C performance marketers, the same buyer as Galvor. It is the closest precedent and also the incumbent whose category language Galvor will be compared against.

Naming note: on 16 Nov 2026 motionapp.com pages redirect to runneth.com and the footer reads "© 2026 Runneth". Reports still carry "Motion" in the title and events say "Motion presents". Runneth was launched in June 2026 as a Motion AI product (search only). I found no formal rebrand announcement. Not verified: whether the company has legally renamed itself.

### People
- Reza Khadjavi, CEO and co-founder; Alexander Sloan, CTO; David Berglas, head of finance. Dara Denney (YouTube creator) hired as chief evangelist. Source: BetaKit (opened) https://betakit.com/motion-closes-30-million-usd-series-b-in-bid-to-become-command-centre-for-creative-strategists/ , 19 Sep 2024.
- Not verified: who heads marketing.

### Plays

**1. "Creative Strategist" identity**
- What: Motion positions itself as the "command centre for creative strategists" and builds everything (summit, bootcamp, newsletter, research) around that job title. It did not invent the title; it adopted an emerging one and became its home.
- Evidence (company-claimed): Khadjavi says the role went from "relatively fringe" to "basically the fastest-growing job in performance marketing" (BetaKit, 19 Sep 2024); stated goal to "train the next 100,000 creative strategists" (company blog, opened, https://runneth.com/blog/motion-30-million-series-b-funding-announcement , undated, c. Sep 2024).
- Evidence (independent report of company figures): over 1,000 customers; ARR up 3x year on year (no absolute figure); headcount 25 to 50; $30M Series B led by Inovia, $42M total raised. BetaKit, 19 Sep 2024. Valuation not disclosed.
- Not verified: any neutral measure of creative-strategist job growth.

**2. Free virtual summit**
- What: annual free two-day virtual Creative Strategy Summit with practitioner speakers.
- Evidence (company-claimed): third annual summit (19-20 Sep 2024) "attracted over 17,500 registrants" (company blog, opened). 2026 edition 24-25 June, free, 16 speakers listed; "Over 152,130 performance marketers have attended Runneth events this year" (opened, https://runneth.com/2026-creative-strategy-summit ). The 152,130 figure covers all events, not the summit.
- Not verified: "about 1,000 registrants at the first summit" (search only).

**3. Original benchmark research from product data**
- What: annual Creative Benchmarks report built from customers' anonymised ad data, plus a benchmarks calculator and a machine-readable edition.
- Evidence (company-claimed dataset): "$1.29 billion in Meta ad spend across 578,750 creatives and 6,015 advertiser accounts", 1 Sep 2025 to 1 Jan 2026. Source (opened): https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/ , undated, 2026.
- Earlier report on creative strategists drew on "over $6 billion in media spend" and interviews with six strategists; covered by NetInfluencer (opened) https://www.netinfluencer.com/rise-of-creative-strategists-motion-report/ , 14 Oct 2024.
- Not verified: downloads, leads or pipeline from the reports.

**4. Newsletter and education**
- Evidence (company-claimed): Thumbstop newsletter "read by 50,000 advertising professionals every week" (company blog, opened, c. Sep 2024). An 8-week live Creative Strategy Bootcamp exists (search only).

### Cost to imitate (judgement)
- Benchmark report from own data: medium. Galvor has the data type; a first edition can be small if the sample size is stated honestly. Highest-fit play in this file.
- Newsletter for one job title: low to medium; Galvor already publishes Decoded.
- Free virtual summit: medium to high; speaker recruitment needs a network. A single 60-minute panel is the small version.
- Owning a job-title identity: high, and "creative strategist" is already Motion's. Judgement: Galvor would need a different, narrower identity or should borrow the existing one without trying to own it.

## 3. Ramp (ramp.com)

### People
- Kendall Tucker, head of creative experimentation. Source: Marketing Brew (opened) https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/10/22/ramp-viral-livestream-brian-baumgartner , 22 Oct 2025.
- Drew Pinta, Director of Growth Data. Source: Exit Five podcast #346 (opened, auto-transcript) https://exitfive.com/podcast/inside-ramps-marketing-creative-bets-measurement-and-ai-agents-with-drew-pinta/ , 13 Apr 2026.
- Ara Kharazian, Lead Economist; Ian Macomber, Head of Analytics Engineering and Data Science. Source: Ramp (opened) https://ramp.com/data/how-we-built-the-ramp-ai-index , 14 Apr 2025.
- Exit Five's related newsletter headline says Ramp has no CMO (as printed by Exit Five; not independently verified).

### Plays

**1. Original data as content: Ramp Economics Lab and the Ramp AI Index**
- What: a monthly index of AI adoption by US businesses built from Ramp's own card and bill-pay transactions, authored by a named economist, with published methodology.
- Evidence (company-claimed dataset): "billions of aggregated, anonymized transactions from over 30,000 businesses" (Ramp, opened, 14 Apr 2025).
- Evidence of pickup (company-claimed list): Ramp's Economics Lab page lists Bloomberg, The Wall Street Journal, CNBC, Financial Times, The Economist, New York Times, Reuters, Axios and TechCrunch under "In the press" and says the index is available in the Bloomberg Terminal. Source (opened): https://ramp.com/data , updated Oct-Nov 2026. I did not open individual press articles, so each citation is not verified.
- Not verified: leads or revenue attributed to the index.

**2. Public stunt: Brian Baumgartner in a glass box**
- What: mid-October 2025, the actor who played Kevin in The Office processed paper receipts in a transparent box in Flatiron Plaza, New York, for about seven hours, livestreamed, with a live counter against Ramp's automation.
- Evidence: "a rough estimate of 10,000 in-person attendees"; livestream on X "garnered 380,000 viewers over the course of about a week"; Ramp estimates "at least 85 million views" across platforms. All Ramp estimates as reported by Marketing Brew (opened), 22 Oct 2025. Signups and cost not reported.
- Not in-house alone: the idea came from outside collaborators (Rohan Kumar, ex-MrBeast; production shop Coming of Age) working at Ramp's office for a week (Marketing Brew).
- Not verified: 100M+, 112M and 214M view figures (search only; the last from a Ramp job posting). Duration is reported variously as six to twelve hours.

**3. Super Bowl ad made in a week**
- What: 15-second spot with Saquon Barkley, February 2025, made in about seven days per CEO Eric Glyman.
- Source: search only (Ramp blog https://ramp.com/blog/welcoming-the-nfls-saquon-barkley-to-the-ramp-roster ; production company Even/Odd says it produced the ad, so "in-house" is only partly accurate). No result numbers found.

**4. Measurement discipline**
- What (company-claimed, Exit Five podcast, 13 Apr 2026): budget split about 70% proven channels, 30% experimental; new channels take six to twelve months to graduate; incrementality and geo-lift tests preferred over multi-touch attribution; AI scans of Gong sales-call transcripts for "how did you hear about us", where LinkedIn came up about three times more than Meta although the attribution model showed the opposite.

### Cost to imitate (judgement)
- Data index from product data with a named author and a methods page: medium. Monthly cadence is the hard part; quarterly is realistic.
- Asking "how did you hear about us" on every sales call and form: low. Copy now.
- 70/30 proven versus experimental split: low; it is a rule, not a spend.
- Celebrity stunts and Super Bowl ads: very high. Reject.

## 4. Lovable (lovable.dev)

Included for founder- and employee-led content. Documentation is thinner than for the other three because the main source is a paywalled podcast.

### People
- Elena Verna, Head of Growth; Anton Osika, co-founder and CEO.

### Plays

**1. Building in public through founder and employee accounts**
- What: every employee is expected to post about their own work; growth leans on what the CEO, team and users post.
- Evidence: Lenny's Podcast episode "The new AI growth playbook for 2026: How Lovable hit $200M ARR in one year", 18 Dec 2025 (opened; description only, body paywalled) https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-new-ai-growth-playbook-for-2026-elena-verna . The description says Lovable "hit $200 million in annual recurring revenue in under a year" "with just 100 employees" and that giving the product away free "has become their most powerful growth strategy". Company-claimed.
- Quotes attributed to Verna by a secondary site that does not name its source episode: "Every single employee is expected to do their own marketing"; "For any founder in the first year, investing in paid as the means of growth is a death trap." Source (opened): https://www.growthtalent.org/insights/elena-verna-on-why-growth-is-now-a-trust-problem-and-paid-is-a-death , 11 Apr 2026. Treat as unconfirmed wording.

**2. Community (Discord, ambassadors, hackathons)**
- Not verified. Discord size (160K to "hundreds of thousands") and a 3,000-applicant hackathon appear in search summaries only.

**3. Revenue milestones**
- $100M ARR in July 2025: reported by Tech.eu (23 Jul 2025, https://tech.eu/2025/07/23/lovable-becomes-fastest-software-company-ever-to-reach-100m-arr/ ) and The Next Web (24 Jul 2025). Search only; company-announced figure.
- Later milestones ($200M Nov 2025, $300M, $400M) attributed to TechCrunch in search summaries, with conflicting dates. Not verified.

### Cost to imitate (judgement)
- Founders and every team member posting their own work: low in money, medium in habit. The transferable part for Galvor.
- Giving the product away: not a marketing-team decision; Lovable's results come from a consumer-like product with instant output. Do not attribute its growth to the posting alone.

| Finding | Sample and period | Label | Source | Date |

Not verified, though widely repeated:
- "Company page posts reach about 1.6% of followers" and "company content is 1-2% of the feed", attributed to Richard van der Blom's Algorithm Insights Report 2025 (about 1.8 million posts). I found only secondary write-ups, not the report.
- "Personal profiles get 561% more reach than company pages". Ordinal's article (opened, https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages , c. Jan 2026) cites a LinkedIn post for this and discloses no dataset of its own.
- "Company page reach fell 60-66% from 2024 to early 2026". Ordinal cites an Entrepreneur article citing AuthoredUp; no sample stated.
- "Employee posts get 2.75x impressions and 5x engagement" (Refine Labs) and "3% of employees share, driving 30% of engagement" (LinkedIn guide): cited by Ordinal without sample sizes.

Employee and founder-led content, from company sources already listed:
- Clay: executive programme produced "6 million organic impressions in a single quarter"; one employee post "drove over 4,500 signups" (company-claimed, Clay blog, 10 Jul 2026).
- Ramp: in AI-scanned sales calls, LinkedIn was named about three times more often than Meta as how prospects heard of Ramp (company-claimed, Exit Five, 13 Apr 2026).
- Edelman-LinkedIn B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report 2026: surveyed more than 1,100 executives in seven countries; reported that 89% of decision-makers who shared thought leadership did so in private channels. Search only; Edelman's page returned an error. Not verified.

Inference for Galvor (judgement):
1. The best-sized study that splits the two (Metricool) shows an engagement gap of roughly 1.6x in favour of personal profiles, not the 5-8x multipliers quoted in vendor blogs. Use the modest figure.
2. For a company page under 5K followers, a few hundred impressions per post is the norm (Socialinsider), so the page alone cannot carry awareness. Founder and team profiles plus other people's audiences have to.
3. Documents and multi-image posts hold up best on company pages; plain text from a company page is the weakest format.
4. Links hurt on personal profiles and help on company pages, which suggests: story on the personal profile, link and report on the company page.
