## Key takeaways
- Finding creators is solved. Meta alone now lists more than 10 million, and most tools turn a brief into a list. Picking the ones who fit your brand, are trusted and can sell is not.
- Clean numbers don’t predict fit. Of the creators Galvor checked whose engagement was in the top band for their size, 62% still weren’t right for the brief.
- The work after the shortlist (scripts, reviews, revisions, tracking) still runs on spreadsheets, email and DMs for almost half of brands.

## Monday, 10:15 a.m.

Sam, our growth lead from [Issue #2](https://galvor.ai/newsletters/decode/false-bangers-why-your-best-hook-isnt-always-your-best-ad/) and [Issue #3](https://galvor.ai/newsletters/decode/creative-diversity-low-why-30-new-ads-wont-fix-it/), needs twenty creators for a kids’ nutrition launch. The influencer tool delivers: mom creators, India, 50,000 to 500,000 followers, engagement above 3%, fake followers under 10%. Four hundred results, all clean.

The team picks the twenty with the best numbers. Scripts arrive over WhatsApp. Six miss the one claim legal approved, four forget to say it’s an ad, and the fashion mom’s is mostly about her outfit. Three weeks of calls later, the videos go live. Views are fine. Orders barely move.

The creator who would have sold wasn’t on the list: a home cook with 28,000 followers whose comments are full of parents asking what to pack in a tiffin. Her bio doesn’t say “mom influencer”, so the filters never saw her.

> Every number checked out. None of them said who would sell.

## Finding creators is no longer the hard part

Meta’s [Creator Marketing Hub](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/meta-streamlines-creator-brand-tie-ups-with-new-marketing-hub/830593/) now makes more than 10 million creators searchable, and partnership ads [grew 97%](https://ppc.land/metas-ads-with-creators-gain-97-to-a-15bn-annual-run-rate/) last quarter. Indian platforms such as [Qoruz](https://qoruz.com/nexus-ai) take a brief and return a shortlist. The money is following: India’s [₹3,000 crore influencer market](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/kofluence-report-tracks-creator-economy-s-formal-shift/4xrn0g9cve1s8tcd4m0revck5k) is heading for ₹5,000 crore by 2027, and brands say [15 to 25%](https://www.medianews4u.com/15-25-of-brand-marketing-budgets-are-moving-towards-creator-economy-iigc-report/) of their marketing budgets are moving to creators. The question is no longer where to find creators. It is which of them will work for your brand.

## Picking the right ones still is

![Bar chart. 81% of Indian marketers rate creator content very or extremely effective (Nielsen 2026, US 54%). 83% struggle to find the right creators (Goat Agency and Kantar 2025). 13% tie influencer spend to revenue targets (Kofluence 2026).](fig_01.jpg)

Indian marketers believe in creators more than almost anyone: [81%](https://www.buzzincontent.com/insight/indian-brands-rate-creators-highly-can-creators-prove-the-results-12778599) rate creator content effective, against 54% in the US. Yet only [13%](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/kofluence-report-tracks-creator-economy-s-formal-shift/4xrn0g9cve1s8tcd4m0revck5k) tie influencer spend to revenue targets, and [83%](https://www.buzzincontent.com/insight/brands-shift-focus-from-follower-count-to-content-quality-as-influencer-spend-soars-9354080) struggle to find the right talent.

Part of the reason is what gets paid for. In [CreatorIQ’s survey](https://www.netinfluencer.com/67-percent-of-creators-earn-under-10000-usd-a-year-while-pay-still-favors-reach-over-engagement-study-finds/) of 5,000 creators, brands ranked follower count last of eight factors, yet it was still the number most closely tied to what creators earn. Brands say they want fit. They still pay for reach. And reach doesn’t sell on its own: a [Journal of Marketing study](https://wwz.unibas.ch/de/newsdetails/revenue-generation-through-influencer-marketing/) of 1.88 million purchases found campaigns aimed at smaller creators returned far more than those aimed at the biggest names.

## A clean account is not a trusted one

Fake followers are the problem every tool already solves, and the most reported kind of influencer fraud: [56.5% of cases](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/) in Influencer Marketing Hub’s 2026 benchmark. But a real account can still be the wrong one. [ASCI processed 1,609 influencer ads](https://www.medianews4u.com/97-3-of-ads-scrutinised-for-potential-violations-were-on-digital-media-asci/) last year and found 97.3% needed changes: real creators, real audiences, missing disclosures or making claims they shouldn’t. Yet [only half of teams](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/trust-gap-grows-in-influencer-marketing-kolsquare-finds) spend even 30 minutes vetting a creator.

A fake-follower score tells you an audience exists. It doesn’t tell you whether the creator talks about your buyer’s problem, whether their audience trusts them, or whether they can make someone buy.

From August to early November 2026, Galvor Scout checked 8,673 creators against campaign briefs, reading their posts, videos and comments.

![Galvor data, 8,673 creator checks, August to November 2026. 62% of 1,018 creators with top engagement for their size still weren't right for the brief. 38% of 1,428 creators whose content looked like a strong fit were moved down on engagement. 66% of 2,586 creators turned down missed a stated must-have. For 76% of the 6,450 creators rated weak or turned down, the lowest score was fit with the brief. Scale: 44,623 creators found, 100,583 posts read, 9,105 full videos, 171,720 comments, 1,193 script sets.](fig_02.jpg)

Among creators with the best engagement for their size, 62% still weren’t right for the brief, and half the group scored below 50 out of 100 on fit with the brief or the brand’s tone. Clean numbers got them onto the list. Their content didn’t keep them there. It cuts the other way too: 38% of creators whose content looked right were moved down because their engagement didn’t hold up for their size. You need both, and only one shows up in a filter.

Scout reads creators the way a person would, at a scale no team can: in that period it read more than 100,000 posts, listened to 9,105 full videos and went through 171,720 comments, then wrote 1,193 sets of scripts for the creators brands shortlisted. Most brands still do this by hand: in [LTK and Northwestern’s October study](https://www.netinfluencer.com/over-90-percent-of-brands-now-run-always-on-creator-programs-study-finds/), 45% run creators on spreadsheets, email and DMs, and [eight in ten](https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/state-of-stafety-creator-marketing-report) use more than one tool just to vet them.

## What today’s platforms do, and where they stop

The tools in this market are good at what they were built for. We read the product pages and help docs of seven. They are fast: Modash and HypeAuditor return results in seconds from their own databases of [380 million](https://www.modash.io) and [227 million](https://hypeauditor.com/discovery) profiles. They find at scale, with filters and AI search over images and captions ([Modash](https://www.modash.io), [HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com)). Fake-follower scores are standard. [Qoruz’s Nexus](https://qoruz.com/nexus-ai) and HypeAuditor’s AI Scout turn a brief into a list with reasons, [CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/brand-safety) checks past posts for brand safety, and several track sales through codes and links.

![Comparison table of Qoruz, Modash, Cloutflow, HypeAuditor, CreatorIQ and Galvor Scout in four stages. Identify: where the search runs (the others search their own databases of 300,000 to 380 million profiles; Galvor Scout searches Instagram live for each brief), time to a list (the others seconds or near-instant; Galvor Scout usually under 2 hours), filters and fake-follower scores, brief to shortlist, finding creators by what they say in their videos. Match your winners: lookalikes. Brief and vet: script and hook ideas per creator, AI check of a draft with timestamps, ASCI and #ad checks. Pay and measure: sales tracking and creator payments, which Galvor Scout does not do.](fig_03.jpg)

Galvor Scout doesn’t do everything these tools do. It doesn’t track sales through codes and links, and it doesn’t pay creators or handle their invoices. If you run those through Modash, HypeAuditor or CreatorIQ today, keep doing so. Where the other tools stop is the part that decides whether a campaign sells:

- None documents finding creators by what they say in their videos. Modash’s AI search, for example, [reads images and captions](https://www.modash.io/engineering/challenges-of-building-ai-discovery), not speech.
- None offers, as a standard feature, an AI check of a creator’s draft against your brief with timestamped notes. Draft approval at Qoruz and HypeAuditor is manual; CreatorIQ is testing an AI version by invitation.
- None documents checking drafts for ASCI or #ad disclosure before posting.
- Their lookalikes match on audience, topics or visual style, not on the reason a creator worked.

### 1 From a search box to your brief

Scout starts from your campaign brief: product, buyer, objective, positioning and must-haves. It finds creators against that brief, not a category tag, and gives the reason each one fits.

It is slower than a database search, on purpose. Instead of looking up a stored profile, Scout searches Instagram live for each brief and reads every candidate’s latest posts, a median of four minutes after finding them. A database can lag: Modash, for example, [refreshes less-viewed profiles](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/5467354) only when someone opens them. Across the last 32 briefs, the first list arrived a median of 49 minutes after the brief was uploaded, and within two hours for 25 of them.

## The shift for marketers and leaders

Five moves Galvor recommends, whatever tool you use.

- **Brief for the buyer, not the follower count.** Write who the creator must reach, what that buyer must believe, and the claims you can’t make.
- **Search by conversation, not category.** Find the people already talking about your buyer’s problem. They are often smaller, cheaper and more trusted.
- **Read the comments before the counts.** Questions and “where do I buy this?” are trust you can see. Hearts alone are not.
- **Fix the script, not the video.** A fix in a script costs one message. In a finished video it costs a reshoot and a week.
- **Judge creators on what they sold.** Give each one a code or link from day one and rebook on orders, not views. Only 13% of Indian brands do.
