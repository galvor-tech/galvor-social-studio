Every creator campaign should make the next one cheaper. Most make it start from zero.

Here's how it usually goes. A brand works with dozens of creators in a quarter. A few videos work. The campaign ends, the agency sends a report, and the next quarter starts with a fresh shortlist and a fresh brief.

What was learned about which creators fit, which hooks held, which settings felt real to the buyer? It sits in a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group and two people's memories.

That's the expensive part. Not the creator fees. The relearning.

What should carry over from every creator campaign:
→ which creator styles fit which job: proof, desire, demonstration
→ which hooks held attention past the first seconds, and for whom
→ which briefs needed the fewest revision rounds
→ which videos worked as ads, not just as posts

If the next shortlist and the next brief don't start from these, the campaign is paying full price for lessons it already bought.

Indian D2C runs on creators now. The brands that win the next few years will be the ones that remember what each campaign taught them.

Plenty of data. Very little memory. That's the gap.

How does your team carry learnings from one creator campaign to the next?
