“We need AI videos” is a request about the tool.
It says how you want the videos made and leaves out what to make.

Three questions turn that sentence into a brief a team can work from. The first is where the videos will run.

1. Where will it run? Name the placement, since a platform name is too wide. A Reels ad, a product page video and a marketplace listing video are three different films. Length, shape and how early the product must appear all follow from the answer.

2. What must it do there? One job. Stop a new buyer mid-scroll, answer a doubt for someone comparing, or bring a past buyer back.

3. What will it run next to? This one gets skipped. If the new video looks like the ads already running, it competes with your own work. In Galvor's analysis of video ads launched May to Sep 2026, new ads that looked like many running ads got 36% less first-month spend.

Only after these three does the making question come up. Sometimes AI is the right way to make it. Sometimes a creator in her own kitchen is.

Decide the job first. How it gets made is the last line of the brief.
