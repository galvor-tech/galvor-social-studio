Bad news costs least on the day you find it.
My rule in client servicing is that a client should never learn about a delay from the deadline.

A creator falls ill. A first cut fails the check. A date starts to slip. The pull is to fix it quietly and report afterwards. If the fix works, nobody knows. If it does not, the client has less time and less reason to trust the next update.

So the message goes the same day, in writing, and it has five lines.

What happened. One sentence, no cushioning.

What it affects. The date, the deliverable, or nothing yet.

What I have already done about it.

What I need from you, if anything.

When you will hear from me next.

The third and fifth lines carry the most weight. They tell the client that someone is already on it and that they do not need to chase.

Write it even when the fix is already under way. An update that turns out to be unnecessary costs two minutes.

A problem the client heard about early is one you solve together. A problem they find on their own is one they hold against you.
