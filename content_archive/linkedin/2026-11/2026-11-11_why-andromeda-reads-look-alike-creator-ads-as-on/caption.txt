When delivery drops after a Meta update, "the algorithm changed" is the easy answer. In our data, the algorithm is mostly reading the creative we gave it.

Meta has been public about what changed.

Andromeda, its ad retrieval system, scores over a billion ads a second. Meta says that is 100× the 2024 system, with +2.4% conversions on Facebook since 2025.

A system that fast doesn't need six copies of one idea. It needs options that are actually different.

Here is what we saw in Galvor's analysis of every new video ad launched May–Sep 2026 across the brands we work with:

New ads that looked like many running ads got 36% less first-month spend.
Six or more look-alikes launched within three days each got 48% less.
A new message over a familiar first shot got 16% less.
A proven message in a new look got 47% more.

My read, as someone who builds the tagging: near-duplicates behave like one bet. Same creator, same hook, same room, different end card. That is one idea split six ways.

So the fix usually isn't a new campaign structure. It sits in the brief.

Before your next batch, compare your live ads on four attributes:
Hook: do the first 3 seconds say something different?
Cast: same creator, same face?
Setting: home, store, outdoors, studio?
Problem framing: price, proof, routine, gifting?

If two ads match on all four, count them as one.

The "Creative diversity" column started appearing in late August and reached most accounts in the last week of October. Worth opening this week.
