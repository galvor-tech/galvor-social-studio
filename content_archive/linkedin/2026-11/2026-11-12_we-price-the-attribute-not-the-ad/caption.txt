Most teams rate an ad as one thing: it worked, or it didn't. That's where the learning stops.

An ad isn't one decision. It's a hook, a cast, a setting, a pace, an offer and a call to action, all chosen by someone.

When the ad wins, one or two of those choices did the work. When it loses, one or two of them cost you. "The ad worked" can't tell you which.

So we don't score the ad. Galvor tags 50+ attributes in every ad and prices each one against cost per order. The hook gets a number. The creator gets a number. The first three seconds get a number.

That's the difference between a report and a brief. A report says what happened. A priced attribute tells the next ad what to keep.

Every ad should make the next one better. It can only do that if you know which part of it worked.
