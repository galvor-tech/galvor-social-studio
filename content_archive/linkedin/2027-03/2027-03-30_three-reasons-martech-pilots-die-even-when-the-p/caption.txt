Three reasons martech pilots die even when the product works.

I've watched it happen from the vendor side, and I've heard the same story from CMOs about other tools. The software did its job. The pilot still ended in a polite email.

1. Nobody owned the change.
The tool showed what to do. Doing it meant changing how briefs get written, how creatives get approved, how the agency works. That's a change in someone's job. If nobody's job changed, nothing changed.

2. The success metric moved halfway.
Week one, the goal was faster insight. Week three, someone asked about RoAS. Week five, the review was about cost. A pilot judged against a moving target always fails.

3. The pilot was too big to finish.
Every channel, every brand line, every team. It never reached a clear answer, so it never reached a decision.

What helps: one owner on the client side whose job will actually change, one number agreed on day one, and a scope small enough to finish in four to six weeks.

We now turn down pilots that don't have all three. It's slower to start. It's much faster to a yes.
