Approval deadlines aren't bureaucracy. They protect the launch date.

On every campaign I run, there's a moment where the creative is ready and the approvals aren't. The video sits in a folder. The brand manager is waiting on the category head. The category head is travelling. The launch date doesn't move, so the edit window shrinks.

That's when mistakes happen. Not in the shoot. In the last 24 hours, when everyone rushes.

So I now set approval deadlines at the start, in the kickoff email, with names next to them:

First-cut review: the brand manager, within two working days.
Claims and legal check: a named person, in parallel, not after.
Final sign-off: one person, one day.

And one rule: if a deadline passes without feedback, we treat that round as approved, and I say so in writing upfront.

In my experience, people rarely push back on that rule when it's in the kickoff email. They push back when it's a surprise.

It isn't about speed. It's about giving the creative team the time the plan promised them.
