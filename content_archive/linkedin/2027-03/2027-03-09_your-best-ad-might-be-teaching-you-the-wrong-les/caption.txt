Your best-performing ad might be teaching you the wrong lesson.

Every team has one. The ad that carried last quarter. Everyone points to it in planning: make more like that.

So the team copies what it can see. The colour. The creator. The format. The music.

But the ad didn't win because of what's easy to see. It won because of one or two things nobody wrote down. Maybe the buyer it spoke to. Maybe the first two seconds. Maybe the objection it answered halfway through.

We see this pattern across the brands we work with. In the new video ads Galvor analysed from May to September 2026, a proven message in a new look got 47% more first-month spend. A new message over a familiar first shot got 16% less.

Same lesson, both directions: what worked was the message and the buyer, not the backdrop.

Before you brief ten copies of your best ad, ask one question: which part of it actually did the work? If the answer is "all of it", you haven't decoded it yet.

A winning ad is a hypothesis that survived. Treat it like one.
