We stopped calling it reporting. We call it learning now.

It sounds like a word change. It changed how our client meetings run.

A report answers: what happened? Spend, CTR, RoAS, by week, by campaign. Everyone nods. The meeting ends. Next month, the same deck with new numbers.

A learning answers a harder question: what do we now know that we didn't know before? And what will we do differently because of it?

Here's the test I use. At the end of any performance review, ask the room one thing: what will the next ad do differently because of this meeting?

If nobody can answer, it was a report.

Most brands I meet have plenty of data and almost no memory. Twelve months of ads, hundreds of creatives, and the team still argues from scratch about which hook to try next.

January is a good month to change the word. Plan the year around what last year taught you, not only around what last year cost.

Every ad should make the next one better. That only happens if someone writes down why.

What's one thing your team learned from last year's ads that you're carrying into this year?
