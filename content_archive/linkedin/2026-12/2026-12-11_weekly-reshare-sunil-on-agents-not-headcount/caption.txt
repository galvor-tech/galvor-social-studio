Before you give an AI agent a job, list your team's decisions.
Most plans list tasks, which is how the agent ends up producing reports nobody asked for.

Take one week of your marketing team's work and sort it into two columns.

Moving information: pulling numbers, renaming files, building the Monday deck, chasing a creator for a draft.

Deciding: which ad to cut, what the next brief says, whether a creator gets a second video.

In the first column an agent can take the whole job. For the second column, three tests are worth applying before an agent decides anything alone:

Can it be undone? Pausing an ad can be reversed tomorrow. A signed creator contract cannot.
Does it come up every week? Frequent decisions give you enough cases to check the agent's record.
Can it show its working? If you cannot see why it chose, you cannot correct it.

A decision that passes all three can go to the agent. The others stay with a person, and the agent prepares the evidence.

Done properly, your team gets its hours back for the second column.
