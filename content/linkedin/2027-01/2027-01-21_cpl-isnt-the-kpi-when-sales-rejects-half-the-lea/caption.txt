The ad with the cheapest orders can be your most expensive ad.
Ads Manager counts an order when it is placed. Your bank counts it when it is delivered and kept.

For an Indian D2C brand selling cash on delivery, a lot can happen in between. The parcel is refused at the door, or it comes back a week later.

Meta sees none of that. It keeps finding more people like the ones who placed the order.

Here is the check I would run before the next budget review.

1. Export orders by ad for a month that has fully closed, so deliveries and returns have settled.

2. Join each order to its final status: kept, refused or returned.

3. Work out cost per order again, counting kept orders only.

4. Rank the ads again.

Then look at which ads moved. If one drops several places, watch its opening. An offer-led hook and a problem-led hook can bring very different buyers at the same cost per order.

The ad decides who shows up. Rank it on the orders that stayed.
