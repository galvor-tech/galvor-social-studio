By 2028 a CMO will run people, agencies and agents together.

Today most teams run them on separate calendars, and that is where the learning gets lost.

The in-house team has a Monday review. The agency sends a monthly deck. Creators work to a campaign plan. AI tools produce whenever someone asks. Each one learns something, and none of it reaches the others in time to matter.

You do not have to wait for 2028 to fix this. Put everyone on one two-week cycle.

Before the meeting, agents do the reading: what ran, what it cost per order, what looked different about the ads that did well.

In the meeting, people decide. What to scale, what to cut, what to test next.

Within two days, those decisions go into the briefs for the agency and the creators, in the same words.

Two weeks later the cycle opens with one question: what did the last one teach us?

Two weeks is long enough for an ad to show a result and short enough that the lesson is still usable.

The CMO's job in that system is to keep the rhythm, and to make sure nothing learned in one corner stays there.
