“I don't like it” is a feeling, and nobody can edit a feeling.
Whoever carries feedback from a brand to a creator has to turn it into a change first.

Passed on as it is, that line costs a full round. The creator guesses, changes four things, and the one thing that bothered the reviewer is still in the cut.

So before feedback leaves my desk, I put three questions to it.

Where? The exact seconds. “The opening” is too loose. “0:02 to 0:05” is usable.

What? The thing on screen or in the audio at that moment. A line, a shot, the music, the pace.

Compared to what? Every “I don't like it” has a picture behind it of what the person expected. Ask for that picture: an older ad, a reference, a line in the brief.

Then write the note as an instruction. “At 0:03, swap the product shot for the line to camera, like the reference in the brief.”

If I cannot write it that way, the feedback is not ready to send. I go back to the reviewer, not forward to the creator.

A creator can act on a timestamp. A mood only gets a guess.
