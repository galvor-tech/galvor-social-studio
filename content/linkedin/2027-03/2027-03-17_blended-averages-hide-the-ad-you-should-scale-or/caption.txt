Blended averages hide the ad you should scale or cut.

An ad set shows a healthy cost per order. Everyone moves on.

Inside it, one ad is doing most of the work at a great cost. Two are average. One has been fatigued for weeks. The average looks fine because the good one is carrying the bad one.

I see this whenever we break an account down to the ad level, and then to the attribute level.

What I look at instead of the blend:
→ cost per order by ad, not by ad set
→ how each ad's cost has moved over its life, not just this week
→ each ad's share of spend versus its share of orders
→ which attributes the efficient ads share: hook, cast, format, offer

The fourth one is the useful one. It tells you what to make next, not just what to switch off.

One more thing. New ads often look worse in their first weeks. In our analysis, some ad styles started about 35% dearer per order in their first two months and ended close to average over their life. Don't cut a new ad just because the blend says so.

A dashboard should end in four words: scale, cut, refresh, test.
