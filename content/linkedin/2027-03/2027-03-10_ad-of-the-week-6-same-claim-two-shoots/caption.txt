Ad of the Week #6: the same claim, shot two ways.

This week I looked at a premium footwear D2C brand running two versions of one message at the same time: you can walk all day without breaking them in.

Version A is a polished studio ad. Clean background, slow product rotation, a model walking on a white floor.

Version B is a creator on a phone at the end of a long day at a wedding, taking the shoes off and pressing the sole with her thumb.

The usual debate is UGC vs polished. Which one wins?

Wrong question. They're doing different jobs.

Version A sells desire. It's for someone who already wants good shoes and needs a reason to pick these.
Version B sells proof. It's for someone who doubts the comfort claim and needs to see it tested.

Here's what I tagged:
Hook: A opens on the product, B opens on a tired person.
Proof: A states the claim, B demonstrates it.
Setting: A is nowhere, B is a moment the buyer recognises.

Running both isn't waste if each one is matched to a different doubt.
