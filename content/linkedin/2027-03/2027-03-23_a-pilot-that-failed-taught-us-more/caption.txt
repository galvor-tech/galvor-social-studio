One of our early pilots didn't work. It taught us more than any smooth demo has.

The brand was a good fit on paper. The team was keen. The product did what we said it would. A month later, nothing had changed in how they made ads.

When we sat down to understand why, the answer wasn't the software.

We had never agreed on the one business question the pilot was meant to answer. We shared insights every week. They were interesting. Nobody owned turning them into the next brief.

So the insights stayed insights.

Three things we changed after that:

→ Every pilot now starts with one written question, agreed with the person who owns the budget.
→ Every insight ends in a decision: scale, cut, refresh or test. If it can't, it doesn't go in the deck.
→ Someone on the client side owns the next brief. If nobody does, we wait before starting.

The last one costs us pilots. Some teams aren't ready to own the next brief yet, and that's fine.

A smooth demo shows the product works. A failed pilot shows whether the team will use it. The second is the one that matters.
