A long-running ad has proved one thing: nobody switched it off.
That is all the Meta Ad Library can tell you about it.

It happens in most competitor reviews. Someone finds an ad that has been live for 60 days, and the room decides it must be working.

The library shows that an ad is active and when it started. It does not show budget, results, or whether the ad sits in a test or a scaling campaign. An ad can stay live because it sells, because it is cheap to leave on, or because nobody tidied the account.

So I treat the duration as a question, and look for three things around it.

1. Versions. Did new cuts of the same ad appear over time? Then someone is still investing in it.

2. What stayed fixed. If the opening holds while the offer and the ending change, the opening is the part they trust.

3. What stopped. Similar ads from the same brand that ended early tell you more than the one that survived.

Even then, what you have is a guess about their ad. The proof can only come from a test in your own account.

It is why we never state a competitor's results from public data.
