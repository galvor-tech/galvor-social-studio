Saying no to a client is part of my job. Making them feel blocked shouldn't be.
I've found the difference is in what comes right after the no.

A client asks for a claim the product can't back up. Or a creator swap the day before the shoot. Or one more revision round the timeline has no room for.

A bare no ends the conversation. They leave with the same problem and a worse feeling about us.

So every no I send now comes with three things.

The reason, in one line, tied to their goal. "That claim could get the ad pulled, and the launch is what we're protecting."

What I can do. A softer claim the brand has approved before. A backup creator from the shortlist. One combined round that covers both sets of notes.

When they'll hear back, with a time.

The client still has a way forward, and they get to choose it.

It took me a few awkward calls to learn this. A no that comes with options feels like help. A no on its own feels like a wall.
