Two weeks ago I shared the five questions I ask before any campaign starts. This is the brief that goes with them.

One page. Nine fields. Nothing a creator or editor won't read.

Who it's for: one buyer, in one sentence.
The moment: when they'd see this and what they're doing.
The reason to buy: the one thing that should make them act.
The objection: what stops them, and how the ad answers it.
Placement: where it runs, the format and the length.
Must-haves: when the product appears, claims allowed, subtitles, language.
What the last campaign taught us: one line, from the data.
Approvals: names and dates.
Done looks like: what we'll measure, and when we'll judge it.

The field most briefs skip is the seventh. It's the one that stops every campaign starting from zero.

A good brief removes ambiguity. It doesn't add pages.
