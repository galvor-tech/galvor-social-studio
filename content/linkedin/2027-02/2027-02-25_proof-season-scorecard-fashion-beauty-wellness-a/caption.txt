Three categories, three anonymised results, one thing in common.
Every RoAS gain came with an attention number beside it.

Fashion, a premium linen and cotton brand: RoAS up 67%, hold rate up 25%.

Beauty, a clean skincare and serum brand: RoAS up 112%, hook rate up 3×, wasted spend down 30%.

Wellness, an Ayurvedic supplements brand: RoAS up 91%, hold rate up 135%, and a 2.6× uplift against aspirational content.

RoAS tells you that something worked. Hook rate and hold rate point to what worked: the opening that stopped people, the middle that kept them watching. That second part is what you can put in the next brief.

So when you judge a pilot, ask for both numbers. A RoAS gain with no clue about the cause is hard to repeat. An attention gain with a clear cause is something the team can build on.
