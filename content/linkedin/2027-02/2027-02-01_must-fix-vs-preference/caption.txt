Every round of feedback needs two columns: must-fix and preference.
Without them, a creator treats a passing thought and a legal problem as the same size.

Feedback usually arrives as one long list. The reviewer knows which lines are serious. The creator does not, so they either change everything or pick the easy ones.

The test I use to sort a note:

Must-fix means it breaks the brief, the brand rules or the law. A changed claim. The product not visible. The wrong pack. A missing paid partnership label.

Preference means “I would have done it differently”. The music, a word, the colour of a shirt.

Then three steps before anything is sent.

Must-fix notes go first, numbered, each with a timestamp.

Preferences go below, marked “only if it is easy”.

When notes arrive mixed, I ask the reviewer one question: which of these stops us from going live?

That question does most of the sorting. Most lists have fewer must-fix lines than their length suggests.

Share the two columns with the brand as well. It heads off the late “but I asked for that”.

A creator who knows what has to change can change it in one pass.
