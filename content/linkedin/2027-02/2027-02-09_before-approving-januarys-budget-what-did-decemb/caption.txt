Before you approve next year's marketing budget, ask for one page.

It answers one question: what did this year's ads teach us, in rupees?

February is when most plans for the new financial year get drafted. The usual draft is last year's number plus a percentage, split by channel. It says where the money will go. It does not say what the last twelve months of spend found out.

So ask the marketing team for three lines.

What will we stop paying for, and which ads showed us that?

What will we pay more for, and which ads showed us that?

What do we still not know, and how much are we setting aside to find out?

The third line matters most to a finance head. It turns 'testing' from a habit into a named amount with a question attached, and a named amount can be reviewed.

If the team cannot fill in the first two lines, last year's budget bought sales and no knowledge. Next year's plan is then the same bet at a higher price.

A budget that starts from what was learned is easier to defend, and easier to cut well.
