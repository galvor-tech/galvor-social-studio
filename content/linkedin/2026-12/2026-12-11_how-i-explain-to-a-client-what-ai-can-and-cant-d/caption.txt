The first AI question a client asks me is rarely about AI.
It's usually: will this still look like us?

I'm still new here, so I've had to find a plain way to answer. Here's where I've landed.

What our agents do well: read a lot of ads quickly, tag what's in them, draft scripts and versions, and flag things a tired reviewer might miss.

What they can't do: know that the founder dislikes a certain shade of green, sense that a joke will land badly in one city, or decide what the brand stands for.

So I explain the work as a split. The agents do the reading and the first drafts. People on both sides do the deciding.

Then I show the client exactly where they sit. Who approves the script. Who signs off the cut. Nothing goes live until a named person on their side says yes.

That part settles the room faster than any demo. Most of the worry I hear is about keeping control of the brand, and a named approver answers it.
