A pilot without a business question is just a longer demo.

It runs for six weeks, everyone finds it interesting, and nobody can say what to do next.

That is an avoidable way for a software pilot to die. So here is the one-page note I would want written before any pilot starts, including a pilot with Galvor.

Line one: the question, in money terms. 'Which creative choices are raising our cost per order?' is a question. 'Let us see what the tool can do' is a tour.

Line two: the decision the answer will change, and the name of the person who will make it. If no budget or brief moves either way, do not run the pilot.

Line three: the result that would make us stop. Agree it on day one, while nobody has anything to defend.

Then put the review date in the calendar before the kick-off.

A vendor who resists that page is telling you something. A buyer who cannot fill it in is not ready yet, and it is kinder to say so early.

The page takes twenty minutes. It protects the six weeks.
